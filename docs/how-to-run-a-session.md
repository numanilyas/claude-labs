# How to run a session

For whoever is teaching this. Skip if you're here to learn.

## The one thing that determines whether it lands

**Everybody has to type.** A session where you demo and they watch produces
nodding and zero behaviour change. A session where they run it themselves and
something goes slightly wrong produces people who can actually do this.

Budget for that: run it somewhere people have their laptops open, and expect
to spend time unsticking individuals rather than presenting.

## Send this before the session

> Before Thursday, please spend fifteen minutes on the setup page: [link]
>
> You'll need a Claude Pro account and the desktop app installed. If you hit
> anything you can't get past, tell me *before* the session rather than on the
> day — it's much faster to fix in advance.

Setup is the single biggest time sink. If half the room does it live, you'll
lose forty minutes and the energy won't come back.

## Formats that work

=== "Lunch and learn (60 min)"

    Aim: prove it's worth their time. Depth is not the goal.

    | | |
    |---|---|
    | 0–5 | Why you're doing this. One sentence, not a deck. |
    | 5–15 | **Lab 2** together — everyone attaches the CSV and runs it |
    | 15–35 | **Lab 4** — the messy Excel. This is the moment the room turns. |
    | 35–50 | **Lab 6** — reconciliation. Let them find the eight items. |
    | 50–60 | Where to go next, and the rule from Lab 11 |

    Don't try to cover skills or scheduling in an hour. You'll rush the
    Cowork labs, and those are the ones that sell it.

=== "Half day (3 hours)"

    | | |
    |---|---|
    | 0–10 | Setup check, fix stragglers |
    | 10–40 | Labs 1–3 |
    | 40–50 | Break |
    | 50–110 | Labs 4–7 — the core |
    | 110–120 | Break |
    | 120–150 | Labs 8–10 |
    | 150–180 | Labs 11–12, then everyone names one thing they'll automate |

    That last thirty minutes is not filler. People who leave without a
    specific commitment don't come back to this.

=== "Self-paced"

    Send the link and check in after a week. Ask what they built, not whether
    they finished.

    Works better than you'd expect for engineers, worse for people who need
    permission to spend the time. For the second group, book the time.

## Where people get stuck

| Symptom | Cause |
|---|---|
| "It just described the spreadsheet, didn't make one" | Code execution is off. **Settings → Capabilities.** |
| "I don't have Cowork" | They're on web, or on Free. Desktop app, paid plan. |
| "My numbers don't match the lab" | Usually they edited the sample file. Re-download. |
| "It didn't use my skill" | The description doesn't say *when* to use it. Rewrite the description, not the body. |
| "It's using up my limit fast" | Correct, Cowork does that. Show them **Settings → Usage** rather than letting them worry. |

## Running the reconciliation lab

Lab 6 is the best one and the easiest to spoil.

Let them struggle for ten minutes before you say anything. The eight items are
findable and the satisfaction of getting to 415,735.30 is doing real
pedagogical work — announce the answer early and it becomes a demo.

The point to land afterwards is not "Claude can reconcile." It's that the
exercise surfaced a bounced 3,200 receivable nobody was chasing and a 14,200
duplicate posting. Those are business problems the reconciliation happened to
catch, and that's the argument for doing it monthly.

## Two things to say out loud

**About verification.** Lab 11 is the one people skip because it isn't fun. If
you only enforce one thing, enforce that. A team that got fast without getting
careful is a worse outcome than a team that never started.

**About data.** Say plainly, on the day, what your organisation's policy is for
putting company data into these tools. If you don't know, say that instead and
find out. People will start using this on real files the same afternoon —
better they hear the rule from you than guess.

## After

The curriculum doesn't do anything on its own. What works:

- Ask each person to name one recurring task they'll do with Claude next month
- Follow up in two weeks and ask what actually happened
- Collect the skills people build and keep a master copy somewhere — on Pro
  they can't share them, so someone has to own the canonical version
